We wish to evaluate the integral
$$
\begin{align*}
I&=\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{6} }\ln(\tan x)\,\mathrm{d}x.
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
Brian has already given a nice solution in terms of the trigamma function; I will aim to get a solution in terms of a different transcendental function, namely the Clausen function of order $2$, which for $x \in(0,2\pi)$ is given by
$$
\begin{align*}
\mathrm{Cl}_{2}(x)&=-\int_{0}^{x} \ln\left( 2\sin \frac{t}{2} \right)\,\mathrm{d}t.
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
Firstly, we have
$$
\begin{align*}
I&=\underbrace{ \int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{6} }\ln(\sin x)\,\mathrm{d}x }_{ A }-\underbrace{ \int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{6} }\ln(\cos x)\,\mathrm{d}x }_{ B }.
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
Expressing $A$ in terms of $\mathrm{Cl}_{2}$ is not too difficult, we just take the substitution $x\mapsto \frac{x}{2}$:
$$
\begin{align*}
A&=\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{6} }\ln(\sin x)\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{3} }\ln\left( \sin \frac{x}{2} \right)\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{3} }\ln\left( 2\sin \frac{x}{2} \right)\,\mathrm{d}x-\frac{\pi}{6}\ln2\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Cl}_{2}\left( \frac{\pi}{3} \right)-\frac{\pi}{6}\ln2.
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
For $B$, it is useful to first note that $\mathrm{Cl}_{2}(\pi)=0$, which follows from the famous result
$$
\begin{align*}
\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{2} }\ln(\sin x)\,\mathrm{d}x&=-\frac{\pi}{2}\ln2.
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
Thus, we take the substitution $x\mapsto \frac{\pi-x}{2}$ to get
$$
\begin{align*}
B&=\int_{0}^{\frac{\pi}{6} }\ln(\cos x)\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\frac{2\pi}{3}}^{\pi}\ln\left( \sin \frac{x}{2} \right)\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\frac{2\pi}{3}}^{\pi} \ln\left( 2\sin \frac{x}{2} \right)\,\mathrm{d}x-\frac{\pi}{6}\ln2\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{2\pi}{3} }\ln\left( 2\sin \frac{x}{2} \right)\,\mathrm{d}x-\frac{\pi}{6}\ln2\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Cl}_{2}\left( \frac{2\pi}{3} \right)-\frac{\pi}{6}\ln2.
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
At this point, we note the duplication formula
$$
\begin{align*}
\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Cl}_{2}(2x)&=\mathrm{Cl}_{2}(x)-\mathrm{Cl}_{2}(\pi-x),
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
which after setting $x=\frac{\pi}{3}$ gives
$$
\begin{align*}
\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Cl}_{2}\left( \frac{2\pi}{3} \right)&=\mathrm{Cl}_{2}\left( \frac{\pi}{3} \right)-\mathrm{Cl}_{2}\left( \frac{2\pi}{3} \right)\\
\mathrm{Cl}_{2}\left( \frac{2\pi}{3} \right)&=\frac{2}{3}\mathrm{Cl}_{2}\left( \frac{\pi}{3} \right).
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
The proof of the duplication formula follows from a generalised version of what we did above with $A$ and $B$, so I won't sweat the details. Putting everything together, we get
$$
\begin{align*}
I&=A-B\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Cl}_{2}\left( \frac{\pi}{3} \right)-\frac{\pi}{6}\ln2-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Cl}_{2}\left( \frac{2\pi}{3} \right)+\frac{\pi}{6}\ln2\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Cl}_{2}\left( \frac{\pi}{3} \right)-\frac{1}{3}\mathrm{Cl}_{2}\left( \frac{\pi}{3} \right)\\
&=-\frac{5}{6}\mathrm{Cl}_{2}\left( \frac{\pi}{3} \right).
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
I think this is a particularly nice form, as $\frac{\pi}{3}$ is where $\mathrm{Cl}_{2}$ achieves its maximum on $(0,2\pi)$.

---
For those curious about how the trigamma solution and this are related, it is due to the following theorem for the trigamma function at rational arguments, sort of an analogue to Gauss' digamma theorem for the trigamma function:
$$
\begin{align*}
\psi_{1}\left( \frac{r}{m} \right)&= \frac{\pi^{2}}{2\sin^{2}\left( \frac{\pi r}{m} \right)}+2m \sum_{k=1}^{\left\lfloor  \frac{m-1}{2}  \right\rfloor }\sin\left( \frac{2\pi kr}{m} \right)\mathrm{Cl}_{2}\left( \frac{2\pi k}{m} \right).
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
Starting with the trigamma reflection formula
$$
\begin{align*}
\psi_{1}(x)+\psi_{1}(1-x)&= \frac{\pi^{2}}{\sin^{2}(\pi x)},
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
we get
$$
\begin{align*}
J&=- \frac{\sqrt{ 3 }}{72}\left[ \psi_{1}\left( \frac{1}{6} \right)-\psi_{1}\left( \frac{5}{6} \right) \right]\\
&=-\frac{\sqrt{ 3 }}{72}\left[ 2\psi_{1}\left( \frac{1}{6} \right)-4\pi^{2} \right] \\
&=-\frac{\sqrt{ 3 }}{36}\left[ \psi_{1}\left( \frac{1}{6} \right)-2\pi^{2} \right].
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
Then we apply the theorem and get
$$
\begin{align*}
J&=-\frac{\sqrt{ 3 }}{36}\left[ -2\pi^{2}+2\pi^{2}+12\sum_{k=1}^{2} \sin\left( \frac{\pi k}{3} \right)\mathrm{Cl}_{2}\left( \frac{\pi k}{3} \right) \right]\\
&=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{ 3 }}\left[ \sin \left( \frac{\pi}{3} \right)\mathrm{Cl}_{2}\left( \frac{\pi}{3} \right)+\sin\left( \frac{2\pi}{3} \right)\mathrm{Cl}_{2}\left( \frac{2\pi}{3} \right)\right]\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Cl}_{2}\left( \frac{\pi}{3} \right)-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Cl}_{2}\left( \frac{2\pi}{3} \right)\\
&=-\frac{5}{6}\mathrm{Cl}_{2}\left( \frac{\pi}{3} \right)\\
&=I.
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
Hence the two solutions are the same.
