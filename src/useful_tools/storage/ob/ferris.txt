We want to determine the real analytic function whose power series is
$$
\begin{align*}
\frac{x^{3}}{3!}+\frac{x^{9}}{9!}+\frac{x^{15}}{15!}+\dots.
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
Clearly, we have some connection to the exponential function. Let $f$ be our desired function, which exists because the series converges by comparing with the exponential function. Looking at powers of $x$ modulo $6$, we drop every power except the ones that are $3$ modulo $6$. A good setting to describe this in are the $6$-th roots of unity, so let $\omega=\exp\left( \frac{2\pi i}{6} \right)$ be a primitive $6$-th root of unity. Note that for $p$, we have
$$
\begin{align*}
\sum_{k=0}^{5}\omega^{kp}&=\begin{cases}
6 & p \equiv0 \pmod 6 \\
0 & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
Hence, we can write
$$
\begin{align*}
\frac{1}{6}\sum_{k=0}^{5}\omega^{k(p+3)}&=\begin{cases}
1 & p \equiv3 \pmod6 \\
0 & \text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
Thus we can write (noting that $\omega^{3}=-1$)
$$
\begin{align*}
f(x)&=\frac{1}{6}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \left( \sum_{k=0}^{5} (-1)^{k}\omega^{kn} \right) \frac{x^{n}}{n!}.
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
Noting absolute convergence (again by comparison with the exponential), we can swap the sums to get
$$
\begin{align*}
f(x)&=\frac{1}{6}\sum_{k=0}^{5} (-1)^{k}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{(\omega^{k}x)^{n}}{n!}\\
&=\frac{1}{6}\sum_{k=0}^{5} (-1)^{k}e^{\omega^{k}x}.
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$

To get an expression in terms of real valued functions, we pair the conjugates. We have that $k=0,3$ are both already real valued, and the rest we pair, so we get
$$
\begin{align*}
f(x)&= \frac{e^{x}-e^{-x}}{6}-\frac{e^{\omega x}+e^{\omega^{-1} x}}{6}+ \frac{e^{\omega^{2}x}+e^{\omega^{-2}x}}{6}.
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
By de Moivre's formula, we have
$$
\begin{align*}
\omega^{\pm1}&=\exp\left( \pm\frac{2\pi i}{6} \right)\\
&=\cos\left( \pm\frac{\pi}{3} \right)+i\sin\left( \pm \frac{\pi}{3} \right)\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\pm \frac{\sqrt{ 3 }}{2}i,
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
Hence, noting that $\cos(x)=\frac{1}{2}(e^{ix}+e^{-ix})$, we have
$$
\begin{align*}
e^{\omega x}+e^{\omega^{-1}x}&=\exp\left( \frac{1}{2}x+\frac{\sqrt{ 3 }}{2}ix \right)+\exp\left( \frac{1}{2}x-\frac{\sqrt{ 3 }}{2}ix \right)\\
&=\exp\left( \frac{1}{2}x \right)\cos\left( \frac{\sqrt{ 3 }}{2}x \right).
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
Doing the same thing for $\omega^{\pm2}$, we have
$$
\begin{align*}
f(x)&= \frac{1}{3}\sinh(x)-\frac{1}{3}\exp\left( \frac{1}{2}x \right)\cos\left( \frac{\sqrt{ 3 }}{2}x \right)+\frac{1}{3}\exp\left( -\frac{1}{2}x \right)\cos\left( \frac{\sqrt{ 3 }}{2}x \right).
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
