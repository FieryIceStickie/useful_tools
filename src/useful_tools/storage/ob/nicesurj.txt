We want to find all functions $f: \mathbb{R}^{+}\to \mathbb{R}^{+}$ such that
$$
\begin{align*}
f(x^{2}+f(xy))&=x^{2}+xf(y)\quad \forall x,y \in \mathbb{R}^{+}.
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
Setting $x=1$, we get
$$
\begin{align*}
f(1+f(y))=1+f(y),
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
so $f$ fixes the set $\img(f)+1$. For any $r \in \mathbb{R}^{+}$, the quadratic
$$
\begin{align*}
x^{2}+f(1)x-r=0
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
has solutions
$$
\begin{align*}
x= \frac{-f(1)\pm\sqrt{ f(1)^{2}+4r }}{2}.
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
Since
$$
\begin{align*}
\frac{\sqrt{ f(1)^{2}+4r }-f(1)}{2}>\frac{f(1)-f(1)}{2}=0,
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
at least one of the roots is in $\mathbb{R}^{+}$, and so $f$ is surjective on $\mathbb{R}^{+}$. Namely, this tells us that $f$ fixes all $r>1$. 
Now suppose $r\le1$. Then $\frac{2}{r}\ge2>1$, so setting $x=\frac{2}{r},y=r$ yields (noting that $\frac{4}{r^{2}}+2>1$)
$$
\begin{align*}
f\left( \frac{4}{r^{2}}+f(2) \right)&=\frac{4}{r^{2}}+\frac{2}{r}f(r)\\
f\left( \frac{4}{r^{2}}+2 \right)&=\frac{4}{r^{2}}+\frac{2}{r}f(r)\\
\frac{4}{r^{2}}+2&=\frac{4}{r^{2}}+\frac{2}{r}f(r)\\
f(r)&=r.
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
We can check that $f(x)=x$ is indeed a solution. Hence, the only solution to the functional equation is
$$
\begin{align*}
f(x)=x\quad \forall x \in \mathbb{R}^{+}.
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
