We want to evaluate the integral
$$
\begin{align*}
I&=\int \frac{x \ln x+1-x}{x \ln^{2}x}\,\mathrm{d}x.
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
Noting that $\int \ln x \,\mathrm{d}x=x \ln x-x+C$, we perform integration by parts with $u=x \ln x+1-x$ and $\displaystyle\mathrm{d}v=\frac{1}{x \ln^{2}x}$. This yields
$$
\begin{align*}
I&=- \frac{x \ln x+1-x}{\ln x}+\int \frac{\ln x}{\ln x}\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=x-x+ \frac{x-1}{\ln x}+C\\
&=\frac{x-1}{\ln x}+C.
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
Hence,
$$
\begin{align*}
\int \frac{x \ln x+1-x}{x \ln^{2}x}\,\mathrm{d}x&= \frac{x-1}{\ln x}+C.
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
