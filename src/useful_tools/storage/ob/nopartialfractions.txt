We want to evaluate the integral
$$
\begin{align*}
I&=\int \frac{8t-t^{3}}{t^{6}+6t^{5}+3t^{4}-20t^{3}+3t^{2}+6t+1}\,\mathrm{d}t.
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
The denominator is a polynomial $p(t)$ with symmetric coefficients and even degree, so its roots come in reciprocal pairs (except possibly $1$ and $-1$) and more importantly we can write $p(t)=t^{3}q(t+t^{-1})$ for some cubic polynomial $q(t)$. To get the coefficients, we solve the following linear system for $q(t)=a_{3}t^{3}+a_{2}t^{2}+a_{1}t+a_{0}$:
$$
\begin{align*}
\begin{pmatrix}
1 &  &  &  \\
 & 1 &  &  \\
3 &  & 1 &  \\
 & 2 &  & 1
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}
a_{3} \\
a_{2} \\
a_{1} \\
a_{0}
\end{pmatrix}&=\begin{pmatrix}
1 \\
6 \\
3 \\
-20
\end{pmatrix},
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
which gives us $q(t)=t^{3}+6t^{2}-32$. Thus, making the substitution $x=t+t^{-1}$ gives
$$
\begin{align*}
I&=-8\int \frac{1-t^{-2}}{q(t+t^{-1})}\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&=-8 \int \frac{1}{x^{3}+6x^{2}-32}\,\mathrm{d}x.
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
The rational root theorem gives us a root of $2$, and dividing that out yields $x^{2}+8x+16=(x+4)^{2}$. Now we make the substitution $u=\frac{1}{x+4}$, which yields
$$
\begin{align*}
I&=8 \int \frac{1}{\frac{1}{u}-6} \,\mathrm{d}u\\
&=\frac{4}{3} \int \frac{6u}{1-6u}\,\mathrm{d}u\\
&=\frac{4}{3}\int \frac{1}{1-6u}-1 \,\mathrm{d}u\\
&=-\frac{2}{9} \ln | 1-6u |-\frac{4}{3}u+C.
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$Finally, back-substitution yields
$$
\begin{align*}
I&=-\frac{2}{9}\ln \left| \frac{x-2}{x+4} \right| -\frac{4}{3(x+4)}+C\\
&=\frac{2}{9}\ln \left| \frac{t+t^{-1}+4}{t+t^{-1}-2} \right|-\frac{4}{3(t+t^{-1}+4)}+C\\
&=\frac{2}{9}\ln \left| \frac{t^{2}+4t+1}{t^{2}-2t+1} \right|-\frac{4t}{3(t^{2}+4t+1)}+C.
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
