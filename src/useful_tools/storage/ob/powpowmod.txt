Let $N=2^{2^{2010}}+2^{2^{2009}}+1$, and let $n=2^{2^{2009}}$. Then $N=n^2+n+1$.

We make use of two theorems for the calculations; Euler’s theorem, which states that when $\gcd(a,n)=1$,
$$
\begin{align*}
a^{\varphi(n)}\equiv1\pmod n
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
and
$$
\begin{align*}
ax\equiv a\left( x \bmod n \right) \pmod {an}.
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
For example, modulo $11$ we have
$$
\begin{align*}
n&\equiv2^{2^{2009}}\\
&\equiv 2^{2^{2009}\bmod 10}\\
&\equiv 2^{2\cdot \left( 2^{2008} \bmod5 \right)\bmod10 }\\
&\equiv2^{2\cdot(2^{2008 \bmod4}\bmod5)\bmod10}\\
&\equiv2^{2\cdot(1 \bmod5)}\\
&\equiv4 \pmod{11}.
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
Directly taking the modulo on each step, modulo $13$ we have
$$
\begin{align*}
n&\equiv2^{2^{2}\cdot(2^{2007 \bmod2}\bmod3)\bmod12}\\
&\equiv2^{8}\\
&\equiv9 \pmod{13},
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
modulo $17$ we have
$$
\begin{align*}
n&\equiv2^{2^{2009}\bmod16}\\
&\equiv1 \pmod{17},
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
and modulo $19$ we have
$$
\begin{align*}
n&\equiv2^{2\cdot(2^{2008 \bmod6}\bmod9)\bmod18}\\
&\equiv 2^{14}\\
&\equiv2^{4\cdot3+2}\\
&\equiv(-3)^{3}\cdot4\\
&\equiv6 \pmod{19}.
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
Evaluating, we get
$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
p & n \bmod p & N \bmod p \\
\hline
11 & 4 & 10 \\
13 & 9 & 0 \\
17 & 1 & 3 \\
19 & 6 & 5
\end{array}
$$
Thus $13 \mid2^{2^{2010}}+2^{2^{2009}}+1$.
