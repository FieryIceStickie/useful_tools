Let $x=\frac{m}{n}$. Then we want to show that for $x$ is close to $\sqrt{ 2 }$, $\frac{1}{2}x+\frac{1}{x}$ is closer.

If we perform Newton-Rhapson on $f(x)=x^2-2$ to approximate $\sqrt2$, then we get the recurrence
$$
\begin{align*}
x_{n+1}&=x_{n}- \frac{f(x_{n})}{f'(x_{n})}\\
&= x_{n}- \frac{x_{n}^{2}-2}{2x_{n}}\\
&= \frac{1}{2}x_{n}+\frac{1}{x_{n}},
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
which explains where the expression comes from.

To prove that it does get closer, we can look at the convergence rate of the method. One can derive with Taylor's theorem the following recurrence for the error $\varepsilon_{n}=\alpha-x_{n}$, provided $f''$ is continuous:
$$
\begin{align*}
| \varepsilon_{n+1} |&= \frac{| f''(\xi_{n}) | }{2|f'(x_{n})|}| \varepsilon_{n} |^{2}, 
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
where $\xi_{n}$ is some number between $\alpha$ and $x_{n}$. In our case, this simplifies to
$$
\begin{align*}
| \varepsilon_{n+1} | &= \frac{1}{2| x_{n} | }| \varepsilon_{n} |^{2}\\
\frac{| \varepsilon_{n+1} | }{| \varepsilon_{n} | }&= \frac{| x_{n}-\sqrt{ 2 } | }{2| x_{n} | }\\
&= \frac{1}{2}\left| 1- \frac{\sqrt{ 2 }}{x_{n}} \right|.
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
For $x_{n}$ close to $\sqrt{ 2 }$, this term will be close to $0$, so $| \varepsilon_{n+1} |$ will be much smaller than $| \varepsilon_{n} |$, and in fact we have quadratic convergence. To get precise intervals for where this is true, we solve for $| \varepsilon_{n+1} |<| \varepsilon_{n} |$:
$$
\begin{align*}
\frac{| \varepsilon_{n+1} | }{| \varepsilon_{n} | }&<1\\
\frac{1}{2}\left| 1- \frac{\sqrt{ 2 }}{x_{n}} \right| &<1\\
-1< \frac{\sqrt{ 2 }}{x_{n}}&<3,
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
which is true when $x_{n}\in\left( -\infty,-\sqrt{ 2 } \right)\cup\left( \frac{\sqrt{ 2 }}{3},\infty \right)$. This certainly includes an interval around $\sqrt{ 2 }$, so for $x$ within $\frac{2\sqrt{ 2 }}{3}$ units of $\sqrt{ 2 }$, $\frac{1}{2}x+\frac{1}{x}$ will be closer to $\sqrt{ 2 }$.
