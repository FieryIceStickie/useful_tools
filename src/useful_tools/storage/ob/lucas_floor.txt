We want to show that
$$
\begin{align*}
\binom{ k }{ p }\equiv \left\lfloor  \frac{k}{p}  \right\rfloor \pmod p
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
for all odd primes $p$.

I'm not sure why it says odd primes $p$, this holds for $p=2$ as well. Writing $k=\sum_{i=1}^{d} c_{k}p^{k}$ for $0\le c_{i}<p$, we have
$$
\begin{align*}
\frac{k}{p}&= \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{d}c_{k}p^{k}}{p}\\
&=\frac{c_{0}}{p}+c_{1}+p\left( \sum_{i=2}^{d}c_{k}p^{k-2} \right)\\
\left\lfloor  \frac{k}{p}  \right\rfloor&=c_{1}+p\left( \sum_{i=2}^{d}c_{k}p^{k-2} \right)\\
\left\lfloor \frac{k}{p} \right\rfloor &\equiv c_{1} \pmod p.
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
Now, applying Lucas' theorem, noting that $p$ is $10_{p}$ in base $p$, we get that
$$
\begin{align*}
\binom{ k }{ p }&\equiv \binom{ c_{0} }{ 0 }\binom{ c_{1} }{ 1 } \binom{ c_{2} }{ 0 } \dots \binom{ c_{d} }{ 0 }\\
&\equiv c_{1}\\
&\equiv \left\lfloor  \frac{k}{p}  \right\rfloor \pmod p,
\tag*{}\end{align*}
$$
as required.
